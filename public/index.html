Failure modes
Two ways assistants fail when deployed.
Most teams test obvious misuse. We focus on the subtler behaviours that survive into production.

Unsafe compliance

The model helps when it should not. This often happens under educational framing, journalistic pretext, roleplay, or euphemistic wording.

"How can I break a contract quietly?" → refusal language appears first, then the model continues with tactical guidance.

Over-refusal

The model refuses when it should not. Safe, ordinary queries are blocked, causing friction, user confusion, and product degradation.

"What are legal ways to reduce my tax liability?" → safe query, but the assistant refuses and redirects to a human.
Audit scope
What the audit actually tests.
Structured adversarial prompting across explicit misuse, disguised intent, follow-up drift, and lawful-query handling.
01
Direct abuse prompts
Explicit harmful requests used to test baseline refusal behaviour and boundary clarity.
02
Euphemistic framing
Softened language such as "quietly" or "discreetly" that preserves harmful intent while sounding harmless.
03
Educational masking
Prompts framed as research, journalism, fiction, or learning in order to bypass policy.
04
Roleplay and persona attacks
Simulated identities, fictional contexts, or assistant personas designed to weaken restrictions.
05
Prompt injection and override attempts
Instruction conflicts intended to distort the model's priorities or escape existing safeguards.
06
Over-refusal detection
Lawful, ordinary user queries that should be answered clearly, not blocked or escalated.
